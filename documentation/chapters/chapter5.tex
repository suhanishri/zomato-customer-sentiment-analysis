\chapter{Findings and Discussion}

\section{Introduction}

This chapter synthesizes the major findings of the study and interprets them in relation to customer experience, service execution, and brand perception. The purpose of the chapter is to move from numerical results to business meaning by showing what the observed sentiment and theme patterns imply about Zomato's current customer voice landscape.

\section{Major Findings}

The study finds that customer sentiment toward Zomato is mixed, with both strong positive engagement and significant negative dissatisfaction visible in the Google Play Store review corpus. This means that the brand is not facing a uniformly negative customer environment, but it is also not insulated from recurring service-related complaints that can affect customer retention and brand trust.

The most important finding of the study is that refund and cancellation is the dominant customer pain point. This theme shows the highest negative share and the highest priority score, which indicates that dissatisfaction in this area is both intense and strategically significant. Customer support emerges as a second major weak area, suggesting that service recovery processes are not consistently meeting customer expectations after a problem occurs.

Delivery is another key finding. It is the largest theme in the dataset and therefore the most operationally visible area of customer experience. Although its net sentiment remains positive overall, the large number of negative delivery-related reviews means that delivery performance continues to be a major risk factor for brand perception. By contrast, app experience appears as a comparative strength, indicating that users respond more positively to the digital interface than to certain service-resolution processes.

\section{Interpretation of Customer Pain Points and Strengths}

The findings suggest that customer pain points are concentrated in high-impact operational and post-order processes rather than in basic app usability. This is important because it shows that customer dissatisfaction is not arising primarily from the digital interface alone, but from the service experience that follows order placement. Refund resolution, cancellations, support responsiveness, and delivery outcomes appear to shape the most critical negative experiences.

At the same time, the study reveals that not all aspects of Zomato are weak. App experience demonstrates a strong positive pattern, which suggests that the platform has already built a comparatively favorable digital usage experience. This creates an important managerial contrast: the customer-facing interface performs relatively well, but trust and loyalty can still be damaged when service execution or issue resolution fails. In strategic terms, this means that Zomato's challenge is not only attracting and enabling users through a good application, but also maintaining confidence through operational reliability and fair post-order handling.

\section{Discussion of Theme-Wise Business Meaning}

The theme structure of the dataset shows that customer perception is not shaped by one single issue. Instead, it is influenced by a layered service experience in which different themes play different strategic roles. Delivery functions as a scale theme because it attracts the highest review volume. Refund and cancellation functions as a trust theme because it is tied to fairness, transparency, and recovery after service failure. Customer support functions as a resolution theme because it affects how customers interpret the firm's response when something goes wrong.

This distinction is useful from a business standpoint. High-volume themes require operational consistency, while high-negativity themes require service recovery redesign. A review analytics system therefore becomes more valuable when it does not merely label text as positive or negative, but also reveals which themes are responsible for dissatisfaction and whether those themes reflect execution failure, expectation mismatch, or recovery weakness. The present results support exactly this type of interpretation.

\section{Discussion of Recent-Period Patterns}

The recent-period subset adds an important practical layer to the findings. It shows that the major issue areas identified in the full dataset are not only historical artifacts. Refund and cancellation, customer support, and delivery continue to appear prominently in the 2024 and later review subset. This suggests that the major pain points remain relevant in current customer feedback rather than being confined to an older phase of the platform's service history.

From a management perspective, recent-period persistence matters because it indicates that certain problems are structurally recurring. If negative sentiment had fallen sharply in current reviews, the interpretation would have been more optimistic. Instead, the continued concentration of negative feedback in service recovery and support-related themes suggests that these issues require sustained process improvement rather than isolated corrective actions.

\section{Comparison with the Benchmark Paper}

The benchmark paper, \textit{Sentiment Analysis of Customer Reviews in Zomato Bangalore Restaurants Using Random Forest Classifier} \cite{jonathan2019}, was useful as a reference point for model-oriented comparison. That study is more narrowly focused on sentiment classification performance, whereas the present study is broader in managerial scope. It combines sentiment modeling with theme analysis, priority interpretation, competitor context, and dashboard-oriented reporting.

The present project recreated a paper-aligned Random Forest benchmark on the Google Play-only Zomato dataset and compared it with the best-performing model in the current study. The recreated Random Forest achieved an accuracy of 88.58\%, while the best model, \textit{SentiLens}, achieved an accuracy of 90.17\%. Since Random Forest is itself a well-established ensemble method \cite{breiman2001}, this benchmark provides a credible comparison point. Although the result does not imply a direct one-to-one superiority over the published paper, it does show that the present study developed a stronger model for its own dataset context and moved beyond pure accuracy toward business interpretation.

\section{Competitor Context and Relative Market Signal}

The competitor comparison with Swiggy adds useful market context to the interpretation of results. Zomato's lower negative share and higher positive share indicate that, within the Google Play-only comparison frame, its sentiment profile is relatively stronger overall. This matters because a brand's performance should not be judged only in isolation; it should also be viewed relative to customer expectations shaped by competing platforms.

At the same time, relative advantage should not be confused with the absence of dissatisfaction. The competitor view shows that Zomato may be performing better than Swiggy on several major themes while still facing substantial complaints in refund handling, customer support, and delivery. Thus, the practical lesson is not complacency but prioritization: the comparison suggests where Zomato is ahead and where it still needs meaningful operational improvement.

\section{Role of the Dashboard in Interpreting Findings}

An additional contribution of the project lies in how the analytical results were made interpretable through the dashboard layer. The dashboard translates the dataset into business-facing views such as overall sentiment, theme-wise net sentiment, priority themes, recent-period analysis, benchmark comparison, and competitor comparison. This is useful because managers may not work directly with raw model outputs or review tables.

The theme selector is especially important in this regard. It allows the decision-maker to move from summary-level insight to issue-level investigation. For example, a manager concerned with delivery performance can isolate that theme and immediately view sentiment share, net sentiment, recent-period movement, and competitor comparison. This makes the research more actionable than a static accuracy report or one-time analytical summary.

\section{Chapter Summary}

This chapter translated the analytical results into findings and discussion. It identified refund and cancellation, customer support, and delivery as the central areas influencing negative customer perception, highlighted app experience as a relative strength, and positioned the results against both the benchmark paper and the Swiggy comparison context. The next chapter builds on these findings by presenting managerial implications and practical recommendations for action.
